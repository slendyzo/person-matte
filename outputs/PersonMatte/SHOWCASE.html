01 / READ→

Source plate

Decode the original RGB frames in sequence. Keep the frame size, frame rate, and timing aligned with the footage.

02 / SEPARATE→

Temporal matting

RVM estimates the person and carries recurrent state into the next frame. Temporal memory helps the edge follow the shot instead of solving every frame in isolation.

03 / HOLD→

Grayscale alpha

The matte records coverage. The solid core holds the body; softer values describe the transition at hair and other partially covered edges. No binary threshold is applied.

04 / OVER

Composite

Use the matte as opacity and place the foreground over a new background. The checkerboard pass makes leftover room, soft edges, and holes easier to see.

Your everyday workflow

Drop the clip. Get the matte.

In File Explorer, drag a video onto EXTRACT PERSON.cmd. The launcher processes the entire clip and writes a fresh output folder beside it.

Get the extraction launcher

Already in this folder. Double-click it to choose a video instead. Keep the launcher, Python script, and models folder together.

  1. Choose your plate.Drop one or more videos onto the launcher. Processing runs locally on this PC.
  2. Let the render finish.The progress window shows frame count and processing speed. Wait for “DONE” before importing the result.

In the comp.

The movie carries the matte in luminance, not an embedded alpha channel.

After Effects / luma track matte

Import the source and person_matte.mov. Align their first frames. Set the source layer’s Track Matte to the matte layer and choose Luma Matte, with inversion off. In older versions, place the matte directly above the source.

For this test, trim the source to 01:39.500 before aligning it with the matte’s frame zero. A full-clip extraction aligns from the beginning of the source.

Fusion / luminance to mask

Feed the matte into a Bitmap mask and select its luminance channel. Use that mask to control the foreground’s Merge over your replacement background. Keep the plate and matte on the same frame range.

Treat this as coverage data: avoid creative grades or gamma changes on the matte. Its ProRes video-range values should decode to normalized black and white in your compositor.

Output=Foreground × α+Background × (1 − α)

Judge the edge in motion. The test retains a little vent texture near the upper-left hairline and a thin light fringe against the dark checkerboard. Check these areas in motion, along with shoulder contours and flicker. Treat this as a useful automatic starting matte; demanding composites may need edge cleanup. The checkerboard demo uses the model’s foreground-color estimate; applying only the matte to the original plate may leave more original background color at the edge. The preview is compressed and reduced in size; use the native ProRes master for compositing.
